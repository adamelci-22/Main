## C1. Gate 1 — RVOL → Opening Range Breakout (volume-confirmed) → selection score (v3.109)

**v3.76 — the three-step test moves from the twenty leveraged tickers to the ten proxies, direct governor instruction, 2026-09-16.** Runs on the Core Ten's ten **proxy** ETFs (C4's Proxy column — QQQ, SPY, SOXX, IWM, XLF, XLE, GLD, TLT, XBI, XRT), not on the twenty leveraged tickers directly. Each proxy is the real, liquid "core ETF holding" for its theme — deep history, no leverage decay, a clean read on the theme's actual volume and range. **It is never itself bought (C4) — read-only input to steps 1–3.** The leveraged long/inverse pair is still the only thing ever entered (E2); this gate now also decides *which side* of that pair. B1's "always profile the exact instrument being traded, never a proxy" is unaffected — it governs the stop/target math on whichever leveraged ticker actually gets entered, a separate step from this gate.

**Step 1 — Relative Volume (RVOL), a 1.0× threshold filter with a top-3 floor (v3.95, direct governor instruction, 2026-10-02, amending v3.93).** For each of the twelve proxies (v3.113): `RVOL = current volume ÷ 20-day average volume at this same time-of-day`. **Any proxy at or above 1.0× passes to step 2; anything below 1.0× is excluded — unless fewer than 3 proxies clear 1.0×, in which case the passing group becomes the top 3 proxies by RVOL rank instead, regardless of whether each individually clears the 1.0× threshold.** The fallback only widens the group (from however many cleared 1.0×, possibly zero or one or two, up to exactly 3) — it never shrinks it: 3 or more clearing 1.0× still means all of them pass, unranked, per v3.93. Proxies that pass under the ordinary (≥3 clearing) case are still **not ranked or distinguished by RVOL magnitude for step 2 eligibility** — a proxy at 1.01× and one at 4.0× are treated identically; the top-3 fallback is the only case where RVOL rank determines step 2 eligibility itself, rather than just breaking a step 3 tie. RVOL's exact values are still reported at every checkpoint and still drive `rvol_decay_pct` (D4/v3.85) unchanged. Direct governor instruction, prompted by live observation at today's 10:35 and 10:40 checkpoints where only TLT cleared 1.0× — a single-name passing group leaves step 2/3 with nothing to compare against and no real test of "is this the best setup on the board," so a floor of 3 candidates guarantees step 2 always has something to filter and step 3 (when ≥2 clear step 2) always has a genuine ATR-expansion comparison to run, even on a quiet morning. v3.93's core simplification — no ranking once a proxy clears 1.0× — is otherwise unchanged.

**Step 2 — Opening Range Breakout (ORB), with direction.** Track **each RVOL-passing proxy's** **9:30–10:00 high and low (the 30-minute opening range, v3.92, direct governor instruction, 2026-10-02, widened from 15 minutes — this is why the entry checkpoint moves to 10:00, C9/D1)** — scoped to step 1's passing group (v3.93), not the full twelve (v3.113). Live price breaking **above the proxy's own 30-minute high** → bullish → the candidate leg is that row's **leveraged long** ticker (C4). Live price breaking **below the proxy's own 30-minute low** → bearish → the candidate leg is that row's **leveraged inverse** ticker. A proxy still inside its own opening range is not a candidate this checkpoint, in either direction. Confirm any break on ≥2 consecutive live reads before treating it as decisive, never a single boundary touch (the same discipline C12/v3.86 already applies to a re-entry). **Widened from 15 to 30 minutes directly in response to a run of fast reversals** — LABD (10/2), TZA (9/30 and 10/1), and SOXS (9/23)'s first trade all broke a 15-minute range and snapped back within minutes, a pattern a longer range is intended to filter without blocking a genuinely sustained break (TLT's 10/2 10:00 entry, held 6+ consecutive closes through the extended window, is the kind of signal this is meant to keep).

**Volume confirmation — part of step 2 (v3.109, direct governor instruction, 2026-10-05).** A confirmed break only counts if its triggering bar had more volume than the 3 bars before it:

```
triggering bar = the first 5-minute bar of the current run that closed outside the 30-minute range
                 (above the high for a bullish break, below the low for a bearish one)
valid only if    volume(triggering bar) > average volume of the 3 consecutive 5-minute bars before it
```

Read on the proxy's own 5-minute bars. A break whose triggering bar fails the test is skipped — not a candidate at this or any later checkpoint while that same run of closes outside the range continues. If price comes back inside the range and breaks out again, the new run's first bar is a new triggering bar with its own test. Report the triggering bar's volume against the 3-bar average whenever a break is taken or skipped.

**From the 11:00 checkpoint onward the volume test is not applied (v3.117, direct governor instruction, 2026-10-06).** At 11:00 and every later entry checkpoint (through 11:55), a break counts if it meets the two-close confirmation alone — including a run whose triggering bar failed the test earlier in the morning (today's XLE: triggering bar 10:10 failed, still outside its range at 11:00). Before 11:00 the test applies exactly as above. Still report the triggering bar's volume against its 3-bar average for every break taken after 11:00, so D4 can compare these entries with volume-passing ones.

**Second and third entries also need the move still going (v3.119, direct governor instruction, 2026-10-06).** Once any position has been entered today, every later entry — a fresh instrument or a re-entry, including C12's T+0/T+10 runs — must meet, on its proxy's 5-minute closes, both: (1) the usual two consecutive closes outside the 30-minute range, and (2) **the latest close beyond the first of the last three closes** (the close two bars earlier): higher for a bullish break, lower for a bearish one. Equal = fails. This keeps the second and third entries out of breakouts that are still outside the range but fading back toward it. The day's first entry is unchanged. A re-entry after a win still needs C12's fresh extreme (v3.86) as well. Report the three closes at entry.

**Step 3 — rank by `selection_score` (v3.109, direct governor instruction, 2026-10-05; supersedes v3.98's room-left rank, kept below for history).** Zero proxies clearing steps 1–2 → correct no-trade. Exactly one → enter it. **Two or more, at any checkpoint → the highest score wins:**

```
expansion       = (today's high so far − today's low so far) ÷ 14-day ATR    -- each proxy's own bars, unchanged
selection_score = (1 − expansion) × RVOL                                      -- RVOL as computed in step 1
```

Enter the candidate leg (step 2's direction) of the proxy with the highest `selection_score`. The formula is applied exactly as written, including when `1 − expansion` is negative (a proxy already past its typical daily range) — the score is then negative and ranks as such. State the top two candidates' scores in the entry report. **(v3.114) Record the top three in E5 at entry** — for the chosen proxy and the next two by `selection_score` among proxies that cleared steps 1–2 at that checkpoint: proxy, direction, `selection_score`, RVOL, expansion, and the proxy price read at that checkpoint. The 12:30 selection log (D3) is built from this record; if fewer than two runners-up cleared, record the ones that did.

**Why (both v3.109 changes):** a replay of this whole gate on all twelve proxies at every 5-minute checkpoint, 9/1–10/2 (23 trading days, 141 breakouts), under the current exits, 3% sizing and daily allowance, across 12 assumption sets (0 / 0.05 / 0.10% cost per trade × 1- or 2-bar confirmation × two trail settings). Current rules averaged −0.05% a day (worst set −0.39%). Volume confirmation: +0.05% (beat current in 10 of 12 sets). `selection_score`: +0.19% (12 of 12). Both together: +0.14%, with the best worst case of any version, −0.03%. Breakouts passing the volume test won 64% of the time, against 53% for those failing it. Caveats: simulated (leveraged legs modelled as 2× or 3× the proxy's move, entries timed on 5-minute closes), only 23 days, and the same test found the base breakout signal has little edge on its own — live results decide.

**[Superseded by v3.109, above.] Step 3 — decide among however many of the passing group cleared step 2 (v3.98, direction reversed, direct governor instruction, 2026-10-03).** Zero clearing → correct no-trade, nothing further to do. Exactly one clearing → enter it directly, no ranking needed. **Two or more clearing: rank them by room left, not by expansion already used.** `expansion = (today's high so far − today's low so far) ÷ 14-day ATR` (unchanged computation, each proxy's own bars), but now `room_left = 1 − expansion`, and **the highest `room_left` ranks first** — the proxy that has used up the *least* of its typical daily range, not the most. **If `room_left` ties or sits within a hair of itself, RVOL rank breaks the tie** (highest RVOL among the tied ones wins) — the only point in the process where RVOL's magnitude (not just its pass/fail threshold) matters. **Enter the candidate leg (chosen by that proxy's step 2 direction) of the winner.**

**v3.98 rationale, direct governor instruction, 2026-10-03: reverses v3.76's original ATR-expansion rank (which picked the proxy that had already moved the most relative to its own typical range — the mechanic that decided Monday's TLT-vs-GLD case) in favor of its exact opposite.** Prompted by the governor's stated goal — a modest, consistent ~0.5%/day base case, not a home-run chase — and the reasoning that by the time step 3 runs, step 2 has already answered "is this move real" (both candidates cleared a confirmed 2-read ORB break); step 3 was only ever choosing between two already-confirmed breakouts, so the deciding question is how much runway is left before each one typically exhausts, not which one already looks hottest. The old rule picked the proxy closest to having used up its typical daily budget — structurally the same shape as this week's fast-reversal losses (LABD, both TZA trades, SOXS's first 9/23 trade all broke out, had already moved, then snapped back). The new rule picks the proxy that's used the least of its typical range, maximizing margin before exhaustion for a small target. Traded off openly: this does not re-introduce a "is this move real" filter (that's still step 2's job alone) and a low-expansion breakout could in principle be weaker/less-confirmed than a high-expansion one — accepted as a reasonable tradeoff for a small, consistent target rather than treated as a free upgrade.

**Re-run fresh at every entry-eligible checkpoint while flat** (10:00 through 12:25, C9) — not just once at 10:00. A proxy that failed step 1 or 2 at 10:00 can clear later as its own volume/range profile develops through the morning; nothing here is a one-shot test tied to 10:00 specifically. Zero proxies clearing all three steps at a given checkpoint is a correct, ordinary no-trade outcome, not a failure to work around.

**Held and managed identically to any other position once entered** — B2's trail, B3's exits, B4's same-day close all apply unchanged; nothing about the ratchet, the stop math, or the close mechanic cares which of the twenty-four tickers (v3.113) is actually held.

**Supersedes C6 (complex confirmation), C7 (`mfe_per_stop` ranking), C10 (momentum/reversal), and C11 (chop filter) for Core Ten entries** — the ORB breakout already answers "is this a real move, not chop," and the ATR-expansion rank already answers "which one has the most room left to run." All four stay in the document, marked retired with a pointer here, rather than deleted outright — same convention as every other retirement in this file (C2, C3).


## C12. Re-entry cycle — an exit restarts the entry clock, not the whole day

**Applies whenever a position closes before 12:30, regardless of why** — stop, reversal, any other B3 exit. **Every step below is still subject to A1's daily entry allowance (v3.101)** — a re-entry when the day's one re-entry is already used, or a fresh instrument when 2 distinct instruments are already entered, is blocked exactly like any other entry attempt (in the latter case step 1's field re-run covers only those two rows); being triggered by an exit is not an exception. (The 12:30 close itself is a direct market sell, v3.58/v3.92, not a stop trigger — nothing re-enters after it, since 12:25 is the end of the entry window, C9, v3.92.) The moment of exit becomes an ad hoc **"opening-range-equivalent,"** rather than waiting for the next regular grid slot (the uniform 5-min cadence, v3.64).

**v3.72 note: steps below updated for the Core Ten/RVOL-ORB-ATR system.** "Shortlist" now means the C4 table's fixed leveraged tickers (sixteen since v3.121; twenty when this note was written), always the same set — there is no daily-built list to re-derive. **v3.76: C1 itself runs on the ten proxies, not the twenty tickers directly** — see below. C1's Opening Range Breakout window (9:30–10:00 since v3.92; 9:30–9:45 when this note was written) is fixed for the whole day and does **not** reset on an exit — unlike the old baseline system, there is no per-candidate baseline to recompute at the fill timestamp, so that mechanic (old step 3) is gone outright, not just renamed.

0. **A profitable exit checks its own instrument first — a stop is a pause to reassess, not a verdict (v3.67).** If the exit's `pnl_pct_position` was positive, before anything else in this mini-cycle, run C1's three steps (RVOL/ORB/ATR) on that row's proxy, alone, checking specifically whether it's still breaking the same direction (long or inverse) as the leg that just closed. **Still clearing all three, same direction, and the proxy has printed a fresh extreme beyond the just-closed trade's own high-water mark (v3.86, below)** — still a valid breakout, not reversed — **re-enter that same leg directly**, sized fresh off current settled cash (C8), without waiting to rank it against the other nine proxies. Only fall through to step 1 below (the full field, freshly re-run) if the same row no longer qualifies in that direction, **the proxy hasn't cleared its own prior trade's extreme (v3.86)**, **the instrument is already at its 2-entry daily cap (v3.83)**, **or `rvol_decay_pct` against that instrument's first entry today is ≥30% (v3.85)** — the shortcut never re-enters a name for a 3rd time, on a meaningfully cooled signal, or on a move that hasn't actually extended past where it already got to, regardless of how clean the breakout still looks.

**v3.86 — step 0's shortcut also requires a fresh extreme, not just an unreversed line, direct governor instruction, 2026-09-26.** `signal_extreme` = the proxy's own highest bar-high (bullish leg) or lowest bar-low (bearish leg), tracked from the just-closed trade's entry through its exit checkpoint — bar-based, the same convention B2's `run_high` already uses, never live-spot-based. Before step 0 fires, live proxy price must clear `signal_extreme` by a real margin, confirmed on at least two consecutive live reads — the identical decisiveness standard C1 step 2 already uses to separate a genuine break from a boundary touch (the XLF/SOXX whipsaw history that standard was built from). A proxy that's merely still on the correct side of the original 9:30–9:45 opening-range line, without having exceeded its own most recent extreme, fails this check and falls through to step 1's full field re-run instead of shortcutting back in. Prompted by 2026-09-25's second TMV trade: TLT was still decisively below its $79.3031 opening-range low at re-entry (live $78.875) but had not made a new low since the first TMV trade's own $78.850 trough — the re-entry caught the tail of an already-stalled move and closed as a -0.25% scratch three minutes later. Backtested against every step-0-after-a-win re-entry on record (4 total, the full population since v3.67 launched): the two that had already cleared a fresh extreme at re-entry (RBLX 9/14, +1.86%; TQQQ 9/21, +0.97%) were both real winners; the two that hadn't (SOXS 9/23's third trade; TMV 9/25's second) were the log's worst single loss (-2.61%) and this scratch, respectively — a clean 4-for-4 split. n=4 is thin and this is explicitly a staged rule from a small sample, same discipline as v3.85's decay ceiling — D4 tracks every future step-0 attempt's `signal_extreme` check, cleared or not, to revisit with more data.

**A losing exit's own instrument is excluded from re-entry for the rest of the trading day, full stop (v3.68).** Not merely denied step 0's shortcut — removed outright from the candidate pool for every C1 run today, this mini-cycle's and any later one's, even if its row's proxy would otherwise clear RVOL/ORB/ATR cleanly in that direction. Applies symmetrically whichever of the twenty-four tickers (v3.113) it was. **The same full exclusion applies to any instrument already at its 2-entry daily cap (v3.83)**, for the unrelated reason of entry-count exhaustion rather than a loss, **and to any instrument whose `rvol_decay_pct` (D4) against its first entry today has reached 30% or more (v3.85, direct governor instruction, 2026-09-23)** — a decayed signal is excluded the same as a losing one or an exhausted one, whichever candidate-pool check catches it first. All three exclusions feed the same check, just triggered by different conditions. Compute `rvol_decay_pct` fresh at the moment of any would-be re-entry — C12 step 0, C12 step 1 landing on an already-traded name, or v3.80's discretionary 3rd-entry path alike — using the first entry's already-logged RVOL (E5) against a fresh C1 step 1 read; ≥30% blocks the re-entry outright, state the number either way. Threshold origin, rationale, and recalibration process: D4.

1. **T+0 (the moment the exit is discovered, same turn, no new trigger needed): re-run C1's three steps fresh on all five proxies (v3.122)** (excluding the losing exit's own direction on its row, per the rule above) — the same test any regular checkpoint runs, nothing special about it being exit-triggered except the timing below. **(Skipped entirely if step 0 already re-entered the same leg.)**
2. **T+10 is measured from the exit's actual fill timestamp (from the order response), never from when it happened to be noticed.** Detection lags the real fill whenever the exit fires between scheduled checkpoints — get the real fill time first, then compute `elapsed = now − fill_time`:
   - **`elapsed ≥ 10 minutes`** — the window has already passed. Run C1 immediately, same turn as T+0. No trigger to arm, no further wait.
   - **`elapsed < 10 minutes`** — arm one ad hoc trigger for `10 − elapsed` minutes out (the nearest possible time to exactly `fill_time + 10min`) to run C1 then.
   Enter the top-ranked ticker if anything clears all three steps, exactly as any other entry checkpoint would. This is in addition to, not a replacement for, the regular grid triggers already armed for the rest of the day.
3. **After C1 runs, whether or not a new position was opened, resume the standard grid at its own next slot — not exit-relative.** Exit at 10:40, discovered and gated promptly → the next check is the regular 10:50 slot, then 10:55, 11:00, and onward through the rest of the grid, unchanged.

**Worked example (v3.64 — cadence is uniform 5-min throughout):** a position exits (fill) at 10:32. The 10:35 grid check discovers it; only 3 minutes have passed (`elapsed < 10`), so an ad hoc trigger arms for 10:42 (`fill_time + 10min`) — landing between two regular slots, the normal case under the tighter cadence. At 10:42 C1 runs fresh across the field. Declined → the next check is the regular 10:45 slot, fully normal from there through the rest of the grid — and 12:30 (v3.92) is the close, the last event of the day.

Fires once per exit, not a new recurring cadence. If T+10 finds nothing that clears every gate, the book just stays flat until the next regular grid slot — same as any other declined entry.

**A chance to re-check, never a mandate to re-enter — everything else already in force still binds at full strength.** C5's "no read = no trade" and C9's "never force a trade" apply to the T+10 check exactly as hard as at 10:00; C9's 10:00–11:55 entry window (v3.94) still governs — a mini-cycle triggered late enough that `fill_time + 10min` would land past 11:55 simply finds no entry available, same as any other post-11:55 moment; A1's one-position gate is untouched. This rule only shortens *when* the next attempt happens, never *whether* one is allowed.
